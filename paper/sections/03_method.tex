\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

We apply OPENER \emph{as is} to the legal domain. The detector and the entity embedder are reused from the general-domain system with their weights frozen, so no gradient step is ever taken on legal text. Only the typing head touches the legal side, and it does so differently at each operating point. OPENER-ZS consumes no legal label at all, deriving its prototypes from the type names and refining them on the unlabelled test mentions. OPENER-Sup fits a linear classifier on the frozen embeddings of the target's training mentions, so it consumes legal labels without updating a single encoder weight. We therefore measure how well a frugal, general-purpose open-world pipeline \emph{transfers} to legal NER, not how well it can be retrained on it.

\subsection{Mention detection and embedding}
Spans are proposed by a frozen GLiNER detector \citep{zaratiana2024gliner} (GLiNER-L for OPENER-ZS, GLiNER-M for OPENER-Sup, see \autoref{sec:exp:setup}) and embedded \emph{in context} by Nomic v1.5 \citep{nussbaum2024nomic}, a Matryoshka-truncatable sentence embedder \citep{kusupati2022matryoshka} that we reuse from the general-domain OPENER \citep{anon2026opener}, where it was sharpened by a triplet contrastive step on English, general-domain CoNLL-2003 \citep{tjongkimsang2003conll} spans and an error-driven hard-negative mining pass. Nothing here is legal-specific: the embedding space was never shown a legal type, which is exactly the transfer this paper tests. The embedder is released on the Hugging Face Hub and loaded as-is. No legal-domain fine-tuning is performed.

Formally, the detector maps a sentence $s$ to a set of candidate mentions $\mathcal{M}(s)=\{m_i\}$, each mention is embedded \emph{in context}, and a typing head assigns it a type drawn from the target inventory $\mathcal{T}$,
\begin{equation}
e_i = f_\theta(s, m_i)\in\mathbb{R}^{D},\qquad \hat y_i\in\mathcal{T},
\end{equation}
where $f_\theta$ is the frozen embedder and $e_i$ the $D$-dimensional vector it produces for mention $m_i$, read together with its sentence $s$. We keep the full $D{=}768$. The predicted type $\hat y_i$ is drawn from $\mathcal{T}$, the legal type inventory of the target corpus, which $f_\theta$ never saw during its own training.

\subsection{Entity typing}
\paragraph{OPENER-ZS} The zero-shot head, the open-world default, uses no target labels. Each legal type $t$ is represented by a prototype $p_t$, the $\ell_2$-normalised centroid of the embeddings of the words forming its name,
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\overline{e}_t&=\frac{1}{|\mathrm{name}(t)|}\!\!\sum_{w\,\in\,\mathrm{name}(t)}\!\!f_\theta(w),\\[2pt]
p_t &= \overline{e}_t\,\big/\,\lVert \overline{e}_t\rVert_2,
\end{split}
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{name}(t)$ is the set of anchor words naming type $t$, for instance ``court'' or ``case law citation''. Each anchor word $w$ contributes its embedding $f_\theta(w)$, and $\overline{e}_t$ is their average. Dividing that average by its Euclidean norm $\lVert\overline{e}_t\rVert_2$ gives a unit-length prototype. These anchor words are English for every corpus, which is precisely what the cross-lingual results of \autoref{sec:exp:results} put under stress.

A mention is assigned the nearest prototype by cosine similarity, refined by one pass of transductive spherical $k$-means over the unlabelled test mentions, and \emph{fused} with the detector's own zero-shot label,
\begin{equation}
s_t(i)=\cos(e_i,p_t)+\beta\,g_i\,\mathbb{1}\!\left[t=\ell_{\text{GLiNER}}(i)\right],
\end{equation}
where $s_t(i)$ is the fused score of assigning type $t$ to mention $m_i$, and $\cos(e_i,p_t)$ the prototype similarity above. The detector contributes two quantities, the type $\ell_{\text{GLiNER}}(i)$ it predicted for that span and its own confidence $g_i\in[0,1]$ in that prediction. The indicator $\mathbb{1}[\cdot]$ is worth $1$ when $t$ is exactly that predicted type and $0$ otherwise, so the fusion term only ever raises the score of the detector's own choice. The mention is typed as $\hat y_i=\arg\max_t s_t(i)$. The fusion weight $\beta{=}0.05$ is inherited from the general-domain study and never tuned on legal data, so the detector only nudges the arg-max (\autoref{sec:exp:analysis} sweeps it post-hoc).

\paragraph{OPENER-Sup} A balanced linear classifier (scikit-learn's LinearSVC \citep{pedregosa2011scikit} with \texttt{class\_weight=balanced}, built on LIBLINEAR \citep{fan2008liblinear}) is fit one-vs-rest on the legal target's training labels. For each type $c$, it minimises a squared-hinge objective with balanced class weights,
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\min_{w_c,b_c}\;&\tfrac12\lVert w_c\rVert_2^2\\
&+\,C\sum_i \omega_{y_i}\,\max\!\big(0,\,1-z_{ic}\,h_c(e_i)\big)^2,
\end{split}
\end{equation}
with $h_c(e_i)=w_c^{\top} e_i+b_c$ the one-vs-rest hyperplane of type $c$, of weight vector $w_c$ and bias $b_c$. Here $e_i$ is the frozen embedding of training mention $i$ and $y_i$ its gold legal type. The sign $z_{ic}$ is ${+}1$ if $y_i{=}c$ and ${-}1$ otherwise, and $C$ is the regularisation strength that trades margin width against training errors. The balancing weight $\omega_c=n/(|\mathcal{T}|\,n_c)$ up-weights rare types, with $n$ the total number of training mentions and $n_c$ the count of type $c$. This matters on legal schemas, where a single type can hold well under $1\%$ of the mentions (Court in E-NER, \autoref{tab:legal-datasets}). A mention is finally typed as $\hat y_i=\arg\max_c h_c(e_i)$. Rather than a single label-name match, this carves the embedding space into one region per type, separating types whose vectors sit close together as long as their regions do not overlap. This is the supervised upper bound, and it needs target labels.

\subsection{Evaluation}
We report both \emph{typing-on-gold} (perfect detection) and \emph{end-to-end} protocols, aligning predicted and gold mentions by character offsets with sentinel labels for detection errors, following the general-domain setup. Quality is the Adjusted Mutual Information (AMI) \citep{vinh2010ami} between the gold type partition $U$ and the predicted partition $V$ over the aligned mentions,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{AMI}=\frac{\mathrm{MI}-\mathbb{E}\!\left[\mathrm{MI}\right]}{\tfrac12\!\left[H(U)+H(V)\right]-\mathbb{E}\!\left[\mathrm{MI}\right]},
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{MI}=\mathrm{MI}(U,V)$ is the mutual information between the two partitions and $H$ the entropy. Mutual information measures how much knowing a mention's predicted type reduces the uncertainty about its gold type. The term $\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{MI}]$ is the agreement expected by chance, and subtracting it makes a random typing score near $0$ whatever the number of types, a perfect one $1$. The denominator uses the arithmetic-mean normalisation (the scikit-learn default). AMI is the appropriate metric in the open-world setting because it never requires the predicted type names to coincide with the gold ones. We also report per-sentence latency (p50) and energy (Wh, measured with CodeCarbon \citep{lacoste2019codecarbon}).
